About

Most commentary on AI stops at the first take: what the tool does, and whether that's good or bad. Least Likely goes one step further, to what happens next and what the obvious take is built on.

The name comes from how these systems write. A language model produces text by predicting the most likely next word. This channel looks for the least likely angle: the view the obvious answer misses.

What each episode does

  • Starts from something you already believe about AI at work.
  • Turns it, often with an idea borrowed from outside AI: a writer, a study, another field.
  • Where it helps, tests the idea with an original study. The data is published so you can check the working.
  • Leaves you with one named tool to keep and use.

Who it's for

People who use ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude and other AI tools at work, but don't build them: managers, strategists, consultants, and people in communications, learning, product and operations.

Who makes it

Least Likely is written and narrated by me, Robin Jones. I work on AI capability and adoption. This is an independent channel: views are my own and don't represent any employer.
